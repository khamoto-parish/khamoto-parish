PART 1 • INTRODUCTION

Where is Khamoto?

Close your eyes for a moment. You are leaving Mbale City. The city noise slowly fades behind you. The road opens. Fresh air. Green gardens on both sides. Boda bodas passing. Children waving. In a few minutes, you are no longer in the city. You are in Khamoto.

Khamoto is a parish in Bungokho Central, Mbale District, Bugisu, Eastern Uganda. It was elevated in the 2020s. Before that, it was under Bumageni Parish. Today, Khamoto stands on its own - proud, growing, alive.

And getting here is easy. Khamoto has an accessible road from Mbale City to Butaleja District, coming through Kibiniko, which sits close to the city. You don't need to struggle to find us. The road brings you straight to us. From Mbale, through Kibiniko, into Khamoto, and on to Butaleja - Khamoto is on the way, not off the way.

When you arrive, you will understand quickly what Khamoto is about. People here are farmers. Rice is life here. Land is not just soil - it is identity, it is family, it is school fees, it is tomorrow.

If you visit Khamoto once, you will remember it. Not because it is a big city, but because the people will make you feel at home.

PART 2 • WHERE WE LIVE

Our 5 Villages - One Parish, One Family

Khamoto has only 5 villages. Only five. But each one feels different when you walk through it. Let us walk together.

SOUTH WEST • HEART
Khamoto Village

Khamoto Village is in the South West. It is the heart of the parish. This is where the name comes from. Elders settle matters here. The famous 70-acre story started here.

NORTH • GATEWAY
Kibiniko / Shibinikho

Kibiniko, also called Shibinikho, is in the North. Close to Mbale City. The Mbale to Butaleja road passes here. The first smile you meet.

NORTH EAST • GROWING
Wambewo

Wambewo is in the North East. New houses. Young families settling. If you come back after two years, Wambewo will look different.

CENTRE • MIDDLE
Tumbu

Tumbu is in the Centre. The meeting point between Kibiniko, Wambewo, Nakhalapa and Khamoto. In planting season, women plant rice here line by line.

Nakhalapa - East - The School Village

Nakhalapa is in the East. In the morning, Nakhalapa is full of footsteps - small footsteps. Children from all 5 villages, and even from nearby areas, walk to Nakhalapa because that is where Khamoto Primary School is. Nakhalapa is near Bumageni Swamp, green and alive.

South - Khamoto Bushikori - NOT Part Of Khamoto Parish

Khamoto Bushikori is in the South. It is NOT part of Khamoto Parish. It is our neighbour. We share a boundary, we share greetings, but Khamoto Parish ends before Khamoto Bushikori.

Khamoto Parish = ONLY 5: North - Kibiniko / Shibinikho. North East - Wambewo. Centre - Tumbu. East - Nakhalapa. South West - Khamoto.
PART 3 • POPULATION OF THE AREA

Population - Many People, Living Close Together

Walk through Khamoto in the morning and you will meet everyone. Mothers going to garden with a hoe on the shoulder. Children running to school with books in plastic bags. Elders sitting under trees, watching the village wake up. Khamoto is full - it has a high number of people.

But look closer. Part of Khamoto is swamp, so the dry land where people actually live is small. That small dry land is closely populated. Houses are close together. Roofs almost touch. Compounds touch. A child can run from one home to another in a minute. Neighbours hear each other cooking, talking, laughing. That closeness is why Khamoto feels like one big family.

And who are the people? The youth take the biggest number. Children, teenagers, young men and women - they are the majority. They dig, they plant rice, they operate rice mills, they ride boda bodas, they work in the poultry farm. Adults are in the middle - parents, farmers, workers who carry the families on their shoulders. The elderly are the smallest number. There are few grandfathers and grandmothers who have lived 70, 80, 90 years. But when an elder speaks in Khamoto, everyone listens - because elders carry the history. They remember where boundaries were. They remember who bought which land in 1983.

Khamoto is a young parish. Young, energetic, full of future - and listening to its few elders so it does not lose its past.

PART 4 • WHO WE ARE - OUR PEOPLE

Tribes - Many Tribes, One Community, One Home

Here is something beautiful about Khamoto. Three tribes live here, but if you sit with them at a wedding, you will not know who is who. Because they have become one.

1. Bagisu - The Dominant Tribe

Bagisu are the dominant tribe. This is Bugisu. This is their home. They are known for Imbalu - circumcision - which is not just a ceremony, but the identity of a Mugisu man. They are composed of different clans, each with its own elders and ancestral land.

2. Bagwere - The Second Tribe

Bagwere are the second tribe. They came from Pallisa and other places, looking for land, for farming, for a better life. They are identified by their clans - the prominent Balalaka Abengoma, the family of Tegule Difasi, the family of Mubbala Amos and his brothers, who came from Pallisa, Kakolo Keitisya. Then the Bakomolo, Dongo's clan - James Tatukayo, known as Dongo. Then the Baikomba and other clans. Today, many Bagwere in Khamoto were born in Khamoto itself. They have never seen Pallisa. Khamoto is their birthplace.

3. Banyole - The Minority Tribe

Banyole are the minority. Clans like Banghemba and others. Fewer in number, but respected, loved, and fully Khamoto.

One message: Bagisu, Bagwere, Banyole - three tribes, one Khamoto.
PART 5 • OUR FAITH - RELIGION & CHURCH

Religion - Many Faiths, Living Together In Peace

In Khamoto, you will hear the call to prayer, you will hear church bells, you will hear gospel songs. Muslims, Catholics, Protestants (St John's Church of Uganda, Khamoto), Born Again - all live together in peace. They pray separately, but they celebrate together. When there is a wedding, everyone comes. When there is a burial, everyone comes.

⛪ St John's Church of Uganda, Khamoto - Our Church

St John's COU is more than a building. It is where this community learned to forgive again after years of conflict. On Sunday, the Anglican community gathers here to pray, to sing, to marry, to bury, to reconcile.

PART 6 • OUR CULTURE

Culture - Circumcision

If you come to Khamoto in Imbalu season, you will understand our culture in one day. Drums. Dancing. Crowds. Families cooking. Elders blessing.

They all practice circumcision. Circumcision / Imbalu is now a shared culture for all in Khamoto. Even tribes that did not circumcise before have now adopted circumcision as part of their culture because they live together with Bagisu. Imbalu is no longer just Bagisu culture. Imbalu is Khamoto culture.

Many people here were born in Khamoto itself. Even if their grandparents came from elsewhere, they themselves are children of Khamoto.

PART 7 • SOCIAL LIFE

💞 Social Life - How People Live Together In Khamoto

Social life in Khamoto is simple: your neighbour is your family. When one family has no salt, they borrow from next door. When one family has a sick child, neighbours come. When one family harvests rice, neighbours come to celebrate.

💍 Marriage - Intermarriage, Early Marriage

There is intermarriage between all the tribes here. Bagisu marry Bagwere, Bagwere marry Bagisu, Bagisu marry Banyole, Banyole marry Bagwere, and so on. Families are now mixed. A child born in Khamoto is both Mugisu and Mugwere and Munyole at the same time. That child is Khamoto.

Girls tend to marry at a young age, sometimes below 18 years. Many are those who have dropped out of school. When a girl drops out - because of school fees, pregnancy, family problems - she often sees marriage as the next step. Her education stops. Her dreams stop.

Boys, after circumcision, they perceive themselves to have grown. They feel they are men. They feel they can marry. In this community, someone who is not studying, the moment you finish circumcision you have now become a man. You have to start your life. So a boy marries early, not because he is ready, but because circumcision has made him feel grown - even when he has no job, no land, no money, and has not finished school. Soon he becomes a father before he can provide.

🕊️ Death & Burial

If you want to see the true heart of Khamoto, come when someone dies. The whole community comes. Neighbours leave their gardens. Friends leave their work. People from all 5 villages come to mourn, to comfort, to help, to bury.

The community provides support in form of money, they collect food, they collect firewood to help when they bury. Women bring maize flour, rice, beans, matooke. Men bring firewood for cooking and for the night. Youth bring water, bring chairs, dig the grave, build the shelter. No family buries alone. And on that day, after burying that person, they always tend to put music to bid farewell for that person. After the grave is covered, after the prayers, after the tears - music comes. Singing, dancing, remembering the dead with joy, not only with tears. Music says: We loved you. We buried you well. Go well.

And they have come up with a group called Team No Sleep. When a person dies, before burying, at night, this team solicits money from the community to help when they bury. This group has been formed to help families which cannot bury, which don't have money, to help in the bury of their dear ones. While the family is crying, Team No Sleep moves from home to home, knocking, explaining, asking: Our brother has died. Please help. And people give - 1,000, 2,000, 5,000, whatever they have. By morning, there is money for the coffin, for food, for burial. Team No Sleep stays awake so the family can rest. In Khamoto, no one should fail to bury their loved one because they are poor.
